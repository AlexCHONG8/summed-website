The problem

Two vials, ten steps, two ways to get it wrong

Therapies that pair a drug with a diluent still ask patients and caregivers to do pharmacy work at the kitchen table.

The traditional workflow

Two vials. Roughly ten steps: draw the diluent, inject it into the powder vial, mix, withdraw, expel air, measure, re-check… every dose, every time.

Where it fails

Every transfer is a chance to mix incorrectly — incomplete mixing, wrong sequence, lost volume. And a mislabeled or shared syringe is a wrong-patient event waiting to happen.

The program

Three steps. Then it's just an injection.

1

Mix

The device brings the two components together inside the pen — no vial-to-vial transfer in the patient's hands.

2

Purge

A short, clearly signaled purge clears the pathway — the patient sees confirmation, not bubbles.

3

Inject

A normal pen-injector dose — the part chronic-therapy patients already know how to do.

Traditional two-vial preparation

  1. Wash hands, lay out two vials and syringe
  2. Draw diluent into syringe
  3. Inject diluent into powder vial
  4. Mix until dissolved
  5. Withdraw reconstituted drug
  6. Expel air, check dose
  7. Re-check vial and syringe labels
  8. Attach needle, prime
  9. Inject
  10. Dispose of vials and sharps

eSyrpen Dual Chamber

  1. Mix
  2. Purge
  3. Inject
